**Why each signal is being tracked** (the reasoning behind each script, not just what it does)

- **PASA (`pasa_monitor.py`)** — declared availability is generators telling AEMO their own future plans (planned outages, uprates). It's the earliest possible warning of a supply squeeze, months ahead. In the QED history, this is what you'd have wanted to see coming before Yallourn/Callide-style events, not after.
- **Cap payout (`cap_intraday.py` / `cap_yesterday.py` / `cap_dayahead.py` / `cap_quarter_to_date.py`)** — this is literally the number a $300-strike cap contract pays out on (and, since the ASX rework, the actual contract settlement *price* too). QED reports the same figure every quarter as "cap-return contribution to the average" and treats it as the primary explanatory variable for why a quarter's price moved. Tracking it live — and now genuinely quarter-to-date, not a flat guess — is the continuous version of that.
- **Spot spikes (`spot_spike.py`)** — every extreme price event in 8 years of QED history (Callide, the 2022 crisis, the Dec-2019 bushfire spikes) shows up here first. It's the fastest possible signal that "something happened," before you know what.
- **Interconnectors (`interconnector_monitor.py`)** — regional price divergence (the persistent north-south NEM split, and SA's repeated premium) is mechanically caused by a region being unable to import cheaper power when its interconnector is maxed out. This tells you *why* a region is expensive, not just that it is.
- **SCADA drops / rebid reconciliation (`scada_drop_monitor.py` + `rebid_reconciler.py`)** — outages are the #1 recurring cause of extreme prices in the QED dataset, more than demand growth. This catches the outage itself in real time, then the next morning tells you the generator's own stated reason (forced fault vs. an economic decision to not run) — the difference matters because ageing-fleet economic withdrawal is a *growing, distinct* pattern per the latest QED reports, not the same as a genuine mechanical failure.
- **Predispatch (`predispatch_tracker.py`)** — a forecast breach that then becomes a real spot spike is a stronger signal than either alone; this is corroborating evidence, not a new mechanism.
- **Coal fleet trend (`coal_fleet_trend.py`)** *(new)* — QED history shows the *structural* decline in coal availability (not any single outage) has been the dominant multi-quarter price driver since 2023 — coal fell below 50% of NEM supply for the first time in Q4 2024. A single-event outage alert can't show you a slow multi-week decline; this can.
- **Negative pricing trend (`negative_pricing_tracker.py`)** *(new)* — this is the direct physical evidence behind the "battery growth is eroding cap value" thesis already in your own forward-view notes. Negative-price frequency climbed from 3.6% (2020) to an all-time 31% (Q4 2025) in the QED dataset; tracking it tells you whether that trend is still accelerating, which is exactly what would tell you if cap contracts are getting structurally cheaper for real reasons.
- **Gas spread (`gas_spread_tracker.py`)** *(new)* — **why we're looking at this specifically**: gas is the swing fuel in the NEM (coal mostly bids near its own low cost and doesn't track international prices much; gas is what actually sets the price often, so its cost moves straight through). The gap between what international LNG buyers pay (JKM) and what our domestic gas market charges is the single earliest warning sign of that pass-through — in the QED history, this exact spread widened *before* the 2018 tightening, the 2021 Callide-quarter gas spike, and the full 2022 crisis, in each case by weeks to months. It's a leading indicator, not a lagging one, which is why it's worth watching even when nothing else looks unusual. Right now (per your own Q1 2026 QED notes) there's a live version of this: a Middle East conflict has spiked international LNG, but domestic gas hasn't followed yet — this tracker is what would tell you if that stops holding.
- **Closure watcher (`closure_watcher.py`)** *(new)* — retirement announcements reprice forward contracts the day they're announced, years before the plant actually shuts (Yallourn's 2021 announcement, Liddell's staged exit both did this). You explicitly called Eraring's closure timing "the single biggest event risk for NSW forward prices" — this is what would catch that announcement the moment AEMO publishes it, instead of waiting for the next QED report to mention it.
- **Customer watcher (`customer_watcher.py`)** *(new)* — real-time visibility into a specific portfolio's own assets, independent of everything else above (useful for knowing your own book moved before anyone tells you).
